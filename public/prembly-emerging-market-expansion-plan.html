Executive Summary

The Strategic Imperative

The case for this plan is regulatory, not a fraud panic. On 10 March 2026 the CBN issued Baseline Standards for Automated AML Solutions : 12 standards, 100 requirements : giving deposit money banks 18 months and other financial institutions 24 months to run automated AML systems. Every regulated institution had to file an implementation roadmap by 10 June 2026. That is a dated, enforceable obligation across an entire banking sector, and comparable automation requirements are moving through the other markets in this plan.

Fraud itself is a more complicated story than a strategy document usually admits, and this plan states it honestly. Nigerian financial sector fraud losses did reach ₦52.26 billion in 2024, up from ₦17.67 billion : but ₦31.1 billion of that was a single incident. In 2025 losses fell 51% to ₦25.85 billion, and reported cases have declined for five consecutive years. What is rising is severity per case: FITC recorded ₦3.3 billion of bank fraud losses in Q1 2025 against ₦468 million a year earlier, a 603% increase, while case volumes fell 33.8%. The threat is consolidating into fewer, larger, better organised attacks. That is an argument for behavioural intelligence over volume-based screening : not a claim that fraud is running away.

Prembly sits at the intersection of both forces, with identity verification, fraud intelligence, background checks, and AML tooling already in one platform. Nigeria is the anchor: it is where our regulatory depth, data coverage, and enterprise relationships are deepest, and it is the market that proves the model. But the pattern : rapid digital transaction growth, thin identity infrastructure, fragmented compliance tooling, tightening regulation : is structural across emerging markets, which is what makes the same platform portable to Kenya, Ghana, South Africa, and Egypt with regulatory configuration rather than rebuild.

This document maps the pain in each of ten sectors, identifies what Prembly can package to serve it, sizes the opportunity from published market data with our own estimates clearly marked as such, and proposes a sequenced 24 month expansion path.

Core Strategic Shift

We stop calling ourselves a KYC company. We become Trust Infrastructure Layer.

KYC is a commodity. Trust infrastructure is a platform. The difference is not semantic : it determines pricing power, contract stickiness, and expansion surface. Every enterprise in every emerging market we operate in needs to trust the people it onboards, the businesses it transacts with, and the systems it operates. That is our market, and it does not stop at a border.

Why Now

Regulatory and Market Conditions Favour a Move

One dated regulatory obligation, one structural shift in fraud, and one wave of transaction growth. These are the three forces the plan rests on : each stated with its source.

Regulatory Pressure

The CBN issued Baseline Standards for Automated AML Solutions on 10 March 2026 under BOFIA 2020: 12 standards, 100 requirements, automated AML/CFT linked to KYC data and customer risk assessment. Deposit money banks have 18 months, other financial institutions 24 months. Sanctions for non compliance run to restrictions on business activity and, in serious cases, licence revocation. CBN CMD/DIR/PUB/CIR/001006

FATF Delisting

Nigeria and South Africa were both removed from the FATF grey list at the October 2025 plenary, after completing 19-point and 22-point action plans respectively. Delisting raises rather than lowers the bar: institutions must now demonstrate the controls that earned the exit are sustained. FATF October 2025 Plenary

Fraud Is Consolidating, Not Exploding

Nigerian fraud losses fell 51% to ₦25.85 billion in 2025, and case volumes have declined five years running. But severity per case is climbing sharply: FITC recorded ₦3.3 billion in Q1 2025 against ₦468 million a year earlier, up 603%, while case counts fell 33.8%. Fewer, larger, better organised attacks defeat volume-based screening. NIBSS 2025; FITC Q1 2025

Digital Volume Surge

Nigeria's tier-1 banks processed ₦286.19 trillion in mobile transactions in 2025 : Zenith ₦104.14tn, GTCO ₦72.4tn, UBA ₦51.65tn. Transaction volume at this scale cannot be monitored manually, which is precisely what the CBN standards now require. Bank FY2025 disclosures

Enterprise Demand Outside Banking

Oil and gas, consumer goods, and industrial firms are absorbing fraud losses through procurement and supply chain leakage with no structured prevention infrastructure in place.

AI Enabled Fraud

Synthetic identity fraud, deepfake social engineering, and automated fraud schemes are outpacing legacy verification tools. Institutions need behavioural intelligence, not just document checks.

The Positioning

What Prembly Becomes in the Market

The strongest version of Prembly is not a verification API. It is the enterprise trust layer that large organisations across emerging markets route their risk decisions through.

This positioning is larger than KYC and harder to replace. Once a company builds its onboarding, compliance, and fraud workflows on Prembly, switching is operationally costly. The goal is not to win one time verification contracts. It is to become embedded infrastructure : the way Stripe became embedded in payments, or Twilio in communications.

The ten capability areas below form the full surface area of the Prembly Trust Platform. Not all need to be live at launch in each sector. But the positioning must communicate all of them from day one so that enterprise clients understand they are buying into a platform, not a point solution.

Identity Intelligence

Real time verification across national ID and banking rails in each market : NIN and BVN in Nigeria, Ghana Card, Huduma and KRA in Kenya, SA ID, Egyptian national ID : plus passports and global document types.

Fraud Prevention

Behavioural risk scoring, device intelligence, account takeover detection, transaction anomaly detection.

Compliance Automation

AML workflows, sanctions screening, data protection compliance across NDPR, POPIA and the Kenya DPA, audit trails, regulatory reporting.

Business Verification

KYB : director verification, beneficial ownership mapping, business legitimacy scoring.

Fraud Consortium Network

Cross institution intelligence sharing on bad actors, flagged identities, and fraud signals via FraudLens and FraudBank.

Risk Orchestration

Centralised risk decisioning layer connecting identity, fraud, and compliance signals into a single API response.

Trust Scoring

Composite trust scores for individuals, businesses, and transactions based on layered intelligence.

Onboarding Intelligence

Smart onboarding flows with embedded verification and risk decisioning : reducing dropout while maintaining compliance.

Background Checks

Criminal, employment, education, credit, and sanctions checks : automated, multi country, real time.

Regulatory Reporting

Automated reporting infrastructure configured per market : CBN, SEC and EFCC in Nigeria, CBK, Bank of Ghana, the FIC and the Central Bank of Egypt : plus cross border regulators.

Three Flagship Platform Products

The Highest Upside Builds for 2026 to 2028

These three products are not new categories. They are the highest value packaging of what Prembly already builds : elevated to platform level, cross sector, and network effect driven. They are what separates a verification vendor from infrastructure.

The Trust Graph
Cross sector intelligence layer : People · Businesses · Devices · Transactions · Risk Signals

A cross border, continuously enriched intelligence graph connecting verified identities to known fraud signals, business reputations, device histories, and transaction patterns across every market we operate in. Every verification Prembly runs feeds the graph. Every new client and every new market adds coverage. The more institutions that connect, the more accurate and valuable the graph becomes : and because fraud rings work across borders, multi market coverage is not a nice to have, it is what makes the graph work. This is the most defensible asset Prembly can build. It takes years of data to replicate and cannot be bought off a shelf.

Fraud Consortium Network
Cross institution shared intelligence : Banks · Fintechs · Insurers · Telcos

Built on FraudLens and FraudBank, this network allows institutions to contribute and consume verified fraud intelligence in a structured, privacy compliant way. A bad actor flagged at one bank becomes visible to every institution on the network : in that market and in neighbouring ones : before they can recycle the same identity elsewhere. This is the "Visa risk network for emerging markets" concept : extremely sticky because the value compounds as membership grows, and no member can replicate the intelligence by leaving.

Compliance OS for Emerging Markets
Single platform for KYC · KYB · AML · Sanctions · Onboarding · Audit · Reporting

A unified compliance operating system : one API, one dashboard, one vendor relationship : that replaces the fragmented stack of KYC tools, AML systems, reporting engines, and audit trail software that most enterprises in these markets currently manage across four to seven separate vendors. Regulatory logic is configuration, not a rebuild: the same platform serves a Lagos bank and a Nairobi one with a different rule pack. For multinational clients this is the decisive argument : one compliance system across every country they operate in, instead of one per country. The CBN's March 2026 Baseline Standards mandate creates the immediate forcing function, and equivalent mandates in the other markets follow the same logic.

24 Month Expansion Path

The product is already built. The expansion does not require a new engineering platform : it requires go to-market focus, vertical packaging, and sales resources aligned to sector specific buyers. The sequencing below moves along two axes at once: sector depth and market breadth. Nigeria proves each sector play first; the market extension follows the proof, not the ambition.

Phase Timeframe Sectors Markets Key Action
Phase 1 Q3 2026 Banking · ICT Nigeria anchor Sign first 3 bank consortium members. Pilot SIM Trust with one telco. Implementation roadmaps were filed with the CBN by 10 June 2026, so every bank has already named its approach — the buying decisions land between now and the September 2027 deadline.
Phase 2 Q4 2026 to Q1 2027 Consumer Goods · Industrial Goods · Oil and Gas Nigeria · Kenya · Ghana entry Package existing KYB and background check products as sector specific solutions. Build two case studies from Phase 1 banking wins. Enter Kenya and Ghana through the pan African groups already served in Nigeria : the buyer is the same group risk function.
Phase 3 Q2 to Q3 2027 Insurance · Healthcare · Capital Markets Add South Africa · Egypt Partner with one registrar per exchange for capital markets distribution. Build insurance fraud detection on FraudBank. South Africa's insurance depth and Egypt's banking scale justify local regulatory presence at this point, not before.
Phase 4 Q4 2027 to Q2 2028 Agriculture · Utilities All five · evaluate next wave Approach via public sector partnerships and development finance institutions. Agriculture is a government adjacent opportunity with significant grant funding across all five markets. The critical principle across all phases: do not rebuild the product for each sector or each country. Repackage, reprice, and sell with sector specific language and local regulatory configuration. The underlying infrastructure is already sector agnostic, and the market specific work is rule packs and data source integrations : not new engineering platforms.

Once a client has run enough verifications, the natural upsell is a platform subscription covering the full Compliance OS — AML workflows, audit trails, regulatory reporting, and continuous monitoring. Priced as an annual enterprise contract, typically $35K to $350K per year, with multi country groups contracting at the upper end for a single system across their footprint. Churn from a fully integrated Compliance OS is extremely low.

Layer 3 : Consortium Network Licensing
12 to 24 Months In · Network Effect Revenue · Highest Margin

Access to the Fraud Consortium Network is licensed separately as a membership fee charged annually. This is the highest margin revenue stream — the infrastructure already exists in FraudLens and FraudBank. Adding new members increases value for all existing members, creating natural referral dynamics.

Layer 4 : Sector Specific Data Products
18 to 36 Months In · Trust Graph Monetisation

As the Trust Graph accumulates depth across sectors and markets, it becomes a standalone data product. Insurers pay for behavioural trust scores. Lenders pay for SME credit intelligence. Governments and development finance institutions pay for subsidy recipient verification at scale. Cross border coverage is the part no domestic competitor can match. This layer makes Prembly genuinely defensible against well capitalised global platforms entering any single one of these markets.

Go-To-Market Strategy

How We Enter, Expand, and Retain Across Each Sector

Prembly's go-to-market challenge is not awareness — it is enterprise sales motion. The buyers are Chief Risk Officers, Chief Compliance Officers, and Heads of Procurement, not developers who self-serve via API docs.

Entry Motion : Banking and ICT

The regulatory mandate is the door opener, and there is a version of it in every market. Financial institutions are now obligated to demonstrate real-time AML monitoring. Prembly's Compliance OS solves this directly. The sales motion: executive meeting with CRO or CCO, demonstrate the live workflow aligned to their own regulator, propose a 90-day pilot, convert to annual contract at completion. For banking groups operating in several of our markets, the pitch is one system across the footprint. The Fraud Consortium Network is sold as an add-on after the compliance relationship is established.

Entry Motion : Enterprise (Non-Financial)

For oil and gas, consumer goods, and industrial goods, the entry point is the procurement fraud narrative — not KYC. The language that resonates is not "identity verification" but "how much are you losing to ghost vendors?" Prembly's Procurement Integrity Platform goes in as an audit and risk reduction tool, converts to embedded vendor onboarding, then expands to cover the full supplier lifecycle.

Channel 1 : Direct Enterprise Sales

Dedicated sector sales leads for Banking, ICT, and Energy. Relationship-driven, C-suite access required. Target 2 to 3 anchor clients per sector in Year 1 as reference cases.

Channel 2 : Regulatory Alignment

Active relationships with CBN, SEC, EFCC, and NCC in the anchor market, extended market by market to CBK, Bank of Ghana, the FIC, and the Central Bank of Egypt. Being cited in regulatory guidance is the highest-value marketing for a B2B compliance company.

Channel 3 : Partnership and Distribution

Partner with KPMG, PwC, Deloitte, and EY to be recommended as the implementation tool for compliance advisory engagements. Their regional practices carry us into markets ahead of our own sales presence.

Channel 4 : Exchange and Registrar Partnership

One partnership per exchange or leading registrar gives Prembly access to the compliance teams of every listed company in that market. The model replicates across NGX, NSE Nairobi, the JSE, EGX, and the GSE.

Channel 5 : Developer and API Ecosystem

Maintain and grow the existing API-first self-serve motion for fintechs and startups. This cohort feeds the Trust Graph and surfaces enterprise referrals.

Channel 6 : Public Sector and DFI

Development Finance Institutions — World Bank IFC, AfDB, FMO, British International Investment — actively fund identity and fraud prevention infrastructure in emerging markets. Grant and concessional funding can underwrite entry into slower-cycle markets and public sector deals.

Retention Strategy

Enterprise retention is built at the product level, not the relationship level. The goal is to make Prembly so embedded in the client's operational workflow that removal requires a cross-functional internal project. The three mechanisms that achieve this:

Data Lock In
Audit Trails and Institutional Memory

Audit trails, compliance records, and fraud intelligence built in Prembly cannot be exported to a competitor without loss of historical context. After 18 months of operation, a client's Prembly instance contains institutional memory that is genuinely irreplaceable.

Network Value Lock In
Consortium Intelligence Dependency

A Fraud Consortium member who leaves the network loses access to every intelligence signal contributed by every other member. The intelligence they contributed continues benefiting others. The incentive to stay is structural.

Workflow Embedding
Operational and Regulatory Friction

Compliance teams build internal processes around Prembly's dashboard, APIs, and reporting outputs. Switching means retraining people, rebuilding processes, and re-certifying with the regulator in every market the client operates in that the new system still meets standards. For a multi country group, that friction multiplies by the size of its footprint. It is the most durable retention mechanism available.

Risks and Mitigations

Market, Regulatory, and Competitive Risk

This plan is ambitious, and operating across five markets adds risk rather than only diversifying it. The board should hold management accountable to an honest risk register. The risks below are real : not edge cases : and each has a defined mitigation approach.

Risk Likelihood Impact Mitigation
Well capitalised global competitor enters our markets High High Speed of network building is the primary defence. A Fraud Consortium Network with 10 members is not replicable by a global entrant in under three years. Local data, local regulatory relationships, and local enterprise trust cannot be bought : they must be earned, market by market. We must build the network before the global players arrive with capital.
Enterprise sales cycles are longer than projected High Medium Build the financial model assuming 12 to 18 month sales cycles for anchor banking clients, and longer still for a first client in a new market. Maintain API transactional revenue as the cash flow base while enterprise contracts move through procurement. Do not staff the enterprise team ahead of signed letters of intent.
Data protection rules restrict cross border data sharing Medium High This is the central risk of a multi market consortium. NDPR, POPIA, the Kenya DPA, and Egypt's data protection law each constrain what can move across a border. The network must be architected so that intelligence signals : not underlying personal data : are what cross markets, with in country data residency where required. Engage each data protection authority proactively to co design the framework before launch in that market. A regulator who helped design the framework will not subsequently restrict it without warning.
Currency volatility erodes dollar revenue High Medium Naira, cedi, and Egyptian pound devaluation have all compressed dollar revenue from local contracts in recent cycles. Price enterprise contracts in dollars or with indexation clauses where the client can bear it, hold local currency pricing for transactional API revenue, and treat multi market diversification as itself a hedge : the five anchor markets do not devalue in step.

Competitive Positioning

The Moat in Plain Terms

The Single Most Important Strategic Action

The fraud consortium network is the moat. Nothing else matters as much as getting it to critical mass.

Strategic Conclusion

The infrastructure play is available. The timing will not hold indefinitely.

The CBN's March 2026 standards put a dated obligation on every regulated institution in our anchor market, with deposit money banks due to comply by September 2027. The FATF exits of October 2025 raised the bar rather than lowering it. And fraud, while falling in total value, is concentrating into fewer and far larger attacks that volume-based screening does not catch. Those three facts, not a fraud panic, are what make this the moment.

Prembly already has the product. We have FraudBank. We have FraudLens. We have identity verification, KYB, AML, background checks, and continuous monitoring : all in one platform. What we are deciding today is whether we take what we have built and position it as category defining infrastructure for emerging markets, or whether we continue operating as a capable but replaceable verification vendor in a single market that is about to consolidate.

The five board approvals above are not aspirational items. They are the minimum actions required to activate a plan that is already resourced at the product level. Nigeria is where we prove it. The emerging market opportunity is what the proof is worth. The organisations that define trust infrastructure across these economies in 2028 will be the ones that moved with intention in 2026. This is that moment.
